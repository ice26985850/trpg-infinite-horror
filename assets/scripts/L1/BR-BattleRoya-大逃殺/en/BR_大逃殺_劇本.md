## II. Plot Summary (GM Spoilers)

### One-Line Summary

> 42 high-school students and 6 Reincarnators are sent to a lone island, forced under the "BR Act" to kill each other for 3 days — only the last one (or team) walks away alive. But the Reincarnators have another way out: the Lord God's mission is not killing but survival.

### Full Plot Background

> The world is modern Japan (near-future fiction). The Japanese government passed the "BR Act," randomly selecting one high-school class each year and sending them to a lone island for 3 days of mutual slaughter — each participant wears a remotely detonatable collar, the forbidden zone expands every 6 hours, and rule-breakers die instantly.

The Reincarnators enter just as the game begins — in the school hall at the island's center, where 42 students have just woken from knockout gas and are listening to the rules. 6 Reincarnators are disguised by the Lord God as transfer students inserted into the class. Everyone is issued a random weapon backpack.

GM note: this script is completely different from Train to Busan — the core pressure comes from **human-versus-human conflict**, not zombies. Every NPC student has their own personality, motive, and strategy. Some ally, some betray, some break down. The real challenge is judging which students to trust, when to fight, when to flee, when to cooperate.

### Story Structure Type

**Hybrid**: free exploration across the whole island, but the forbidden zone's expansion every 6 hours creates linear pressure, ultimately pointing to the beach finale.

### World Special Rules

- **Random Weapon Issue**: At start each Reincarnator rolls 1d6 for a weapon backpack: 1=pistol (2d6, ammo ×6), 2=crossbow (1d8+2, bolts ×8), 3=cleaver (1d6 slash), 4=nunchaku (1d4 blunt), 5=binoculars+map (not a weapon, but grants full island map intel), 6=no weapon (can take from others). Reincarnators may trade weapons.
- **Forbidden-Zone System**: A new forbidden zone every 6 hours (island divided into A-1~F-6 six zones). Inside a forbidden zone = collar detonates within 5 minutes (instant death). Zone ranges are broadcast island-wide by the loudspeaker at each expansion, with the dead's number list. GM should announce the current safe zone via broadcast at each expansion.
- **Collar System**: Each contestant's collar has: (a) GPS tracking (authority can track position), (b) eavesdropping (authority hears conversations), (c) auto-detonate in forbidden zone, (d) any removal attempt detonates immediately. Reincarnators cannot remove collars (not an option this script), but S-02 can temporarily slow zone progression.
- **Death Broadcast Every 6 Hours**: The island loudspeaker plays the dead contestants' number list every 6 hours. Players can infer remaining count, who's alive, and which zones are dangerous.

### Lord God Restrictions

- "Do not reveal the existence of the Lord God space to story characters."
- "Supernatural abilities (magic, psionics, etc.) are forbidden in this world."
- "Collars cannot be removed; attempting removal equals abandoning the mission (instant erasure)."
- "The game authority (military) cannot be attacked or threatened — they are off-island, monitoring via satellite."

- **GM Memo**:
  - **Common Stuck Points**: Players may be too passive to ally or fight. Fix — gradually raise pressure via death broadcasts so players realize inaction gets them cornered by the zone.
  - **Difficulty Up**: Increase Kiriyama's appearance frequency (patrol from Day 2), shorten zone interval to 4 hours, add a hidden hostile student (★★, with explosives).
  - **Difficulty Down**: Downgrade Kiriyama to ★★, remove trap students' bear traps (replace with rope trap 1d4 dmg), increase supply discovery rate.
  - **Unconventional Player Actions**: If players try to "fake death to fool the authority" — collars monitor heartbeats, so this fails. GM can call it out via broadcast ("We know who's still breathing").
  - **NPC Behavior Memo**: Shogo Kawada is the most reliable ally — a former champion who knows the rules and inwardly loathes the system. Shuya Nanahara is devoted to protecting Noriko Nakagawa; if she's hurt he loses reason. Kazuo Kiriyama cannot be negotiated, allied, or persuaded.
